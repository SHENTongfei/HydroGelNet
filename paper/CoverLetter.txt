Dear Editor,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript entitled "SIMPLEX: Composition-Space Deep Learning for Bio-inspired Hydrogel Adhesion with Prospective Validation on Model-Discovered Formulations" for consideration as an Original Research article in the Research Topic "Functional Nanomaterials and Interfaces for Biohybrid Systems" of Frontiers in Materials / Frontiers in Biomaterials Science.

The design of functional hydrogels for biohybrid interfaces -- wet, load-bearing junctions between living tissue and synthetic materials -- relies on trial-and-error experimentation. In this work we present SIMPLEX, a dual-modality residual deep network with interaction self-attention that maps the monomer composition of hydrogels (six molar fractions on the composition simplex plus their 15 pairwise interactions) to underwater adhesion strength (kPa). The model is trained on 316 formulations spanning the full experimentally explored adhesion range (1--353 kPa) and prospectively validated on 25 independent formulations discovered in the final round of the original study's sequential model-based optimisation loop -- a genuinely held-out cohort that was never touched during model selection.

Key findings:
- SIMPLEX matches the strongest baseline in-distribution (internal R2 = 0.79 vs random forest 0.81, statistically tied) and attains the highest prospective performance of all models on the 25-formulation held-out cohort: R2 = 0.71, Spearman rho = 0.87, ROC-AUC = 0.94, Top-10 screening precision = 1.00 and Top-20 precision = 0.95.
- The deep model transfers with a small internal-to-prospective generalisation gap (0.79 -> 0.71), whereas random forest degrades sharply (0.81 -> 0.44), demonstrating that continuous, interaction-aware composition encoding generalises to previously unseen, model-discovered formulations.
- Permutation importance yields literature-consistent materials insight: the BA x PEA (hydrophobic-aromatic) interaction dominates, the cationic monomer ATAC and the hydrophobic monomer BA rank next, while hydrophilic (HEA) and acidic (CBEA) combinations are significantly inhibitory -- consistent with electrostatic/hydrophobic synergy and hydration-layer screening effects known in underwater-adhesion chemistry.

All data are public (sheng-hu/hydrogels, MIT licence, Nature 644:89--95, 2025), and all code, processed tensors and per-formulation predictions are released at https://github.com/SHENTongfei/HydroGelNet for full reproducibility.

We believe this work fits the Research Topic by providing a computational screening tool for biohybrid interface materials, and we hope it will be of interest to the readership of Frontiers. The manuscript has not been published previously and is not under consideration elsewhere. All authors have approved the submission and declare no conflict of interest.
