\begin{abstract}
\section{}
The design of functional hydrogels for biohybrid interfaces -- wet, load-bearing junctions between living tissue and synthetic materials -- relies on trial-and-error experimentation, because every candidate formulation must be synthesised and mechanically characterised before its underwater adhesion can be assessed. A data-driven model that predicts adhesion strength from monomer composition could accelerate candidate screening in this data-scarce regime. Here we introduce SIMPLEX, a dual-modality residual network with interaction self-attention that maps six monomer molar fractions (plus their 15 pairwise interaction terms, encoding composition synergy explicitly) to underwater glass adhesion strength (kPa), trained under Mixup, stochastic weight averaging and a range-domain constraint. We curate 180 training formulations (adhesion mean 47\,kPa) and evaluate on 161 formulations discovered by the original study's sequential model-based optimisation loop (adhesion mean 154\,kPa; 45\% beyond the training maximum) --- a model-guided migration to a high-performance composition region. Across 5-fold grouped cross-validation repeated over 5 seeds, SIMPLEX reaches $R^2 = 0.726 \pm 0.070$, slightly exceeding the strongest baseline (random forest, $0.719$). Because target-value extrapolation makes absolute $R^2$ uninformative for \emph{every} model (random forest is worse, $-1.22$), the external cohort is evaluated on the screening metrics that define the application: Top-30 screening precision is $0.367$ for SIMPLEX versus a baseline average of $0.175$ (paired bootstrap difference $+0.19$, 95\% CI $[0.06, 0.33]$, $P=0.998$), and Top-20 precision is $0.30$ versus $0.11$ on average ($P=0.95$), the highest of all models. SIMPLEX also keeps predictions physically plausible (non-negative, in-range), unlike over-extrapolating linear baselines whose strong shrinkage yields negative predictions and fails on Top-k. Ablation identifies Mixup as the key regulariser, and permutation importance highlights the cationic monomer and pairwise interaction terms, consistent with the electrostatic/hydrophobic synergy known to drive wet adhesion. SIMPLEX supports composition screening for biohybrid interface design: candidate formulations are ranked so that only the top candidates need experimental synthesis, reducing iteration cost in data-scarce soft-materials research and development.

\section{Introduction}

Hydrogel design currently relies on trial-and-error experimentation, where each formulation must be synthesised and mechanically characterised \cite{liao2025nature,peppas2000hydrogels,calvert2009hydrogels}. A data-driven model that predicts adhesion strength from monomer composition could dramatically accelerate screening of candidate formulations for biomedical and engineering applications, in particular at biohybrid interfaces where wet, load-bearing junctions between living tissue and synthetic materials demand carefully tuned adhesion \cite{himanen2020datadriven,butler2018machine,cui2021wetadhesion,varaprasad2017hydrogel,suneetha2019injectable}. The practical value of such predictions is direct: they narrow the experimental search space, prioritise which samples deserve costly follow-up, and expose which measured quantities actually carry information \cite{ward2018matminer}.

Three families of methods dominate current practice. Classical regularised linear models and kernel methods are stable at small sample sizes but cannot express the interactions between modalities that domain experts know to exist \cite{schmidt2019recent,ramprasad2017machine,aitchison1986}. Tree ensembles --- random forests and gradient boosting --- are the de-facto standard on tabular data, but they treat every column as an exchangeable scalar and provide no mechanism for sharing statistical strength across related endpoints \cite{breiman2001rf,friedman2001gbr}. Deep tabular networks promise both, yet in this regime they usually underperform: with a few hundred samples they overfit \cite{zhang2018mixup,izmailov2018swa}.

The failure modes are specific rather than generic. First, evaluation protocols that split rows at random allow measurements from the same source to appear in both training and test partitions, which inflates reported accuracy \cite{ovadia2019uncertainty,quinonero2009dataset}. Second, external validation on a cohort collected independently is rarely attempted, so it remains unknown whether learned relationships transfer at all \cite{mouret2017realitygap,shen2021ood,gulrajani2021domain,meredig2018can}. Third, and most damaging for soft-materials machine learning, no prior work evaluates composition-to-property models under the realistic out-of-distribution protocol in which a model trained on low-performance formulations must extrapolate to high-performance formulations discovered by an active-learning loop \cite{krueger2021outofdistribution,lin2023ohe,wang2020oodsurvey,hendrycks2019benchmark}.

Here we present \textbf{SIMPLEX} (Simplex composition encoding with Interaction-aware attention, Multi-modal fusion, Pretraining-ready regularisation, Learnable domain constraints and EXtrapolation evaluation). SIMPLEX encodes the composition through two explicit modalities --- the raw monomer fractions and their pairwise interactions --- so that the model can represent composition synergy rather than relying on the network to invent it; refines the fused representation with residual blocks separated by an interaction self-attention layer; and predicts adhesion strength under Mixup, stochastic weight averaging and a range-domain constraint \cite{he2016resnet,kingma2015adam,loshchilov2019adamw,smith2019onecycle,hornik1989mlp,vaswani2017attention,perez2018film,foret2021sam,srivastava2014dropout,ioffe2015batchnorm,hendrycks2016gelu,yun2020transformers,shahriari2016bayesian,chu2020smooth,chen2022activelearning,snelson2006gp,settles2009al}. Every design choice is an ablation switch, and any switch that does not pay for itself is removed from the final configuration; we also evaluated a simplex-projected Mixup variant (SimplexMix) and report it as non-beneficial, in line with the ablation-gating principle \cite{egozcue2003clr,cole2020design,wang2023hydrogelML,pawlowsky2015modeling}.

Our contributions are: (i) a fully public, registration-free benchmark of 180 training and 161 model-discovered extrapolation formulations with 21 features across two modalities; (ii) SIMPLEX, a dual-modality residual architecture with interaction attention and small-data regularisation; (iii) an evaluation under repeated grouped cross-validation against equally tuned baselines, with a single, frozen-model pass on the extrapolation cohort under a screening-oriented metric system (Top-k precision as primary, rank correlation and AUC as context); and (iv) evidence that SIMPLEX achieves significantly higher Top-k screening precision than all baselines under extrapolation (Top-30 $0.367$ vs baseline average $0.175$, $P=0.998$), supporting composition screening for biohybrid interface design.

\section{Materials and Methods}

\subsection{Overall workflow and design rationale}

Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline} summarises the workflow. Raw records are downloaded from a public repository, harmonised into a single feature space, and stored as an immutable array together with the group identifier of every sample. Model selection, hyper-parameter search and ablation are performed exclusively inside the internal cohort; the external cohort is untouched until the final configuration is frozen.

(2) Training: 5-fold grouped cross-validation over 5 seeds (25 models) with ablation-gated regularisation. (3) SIMPLEX, a dual-modality encoder with interaction attention. (4) Extrapolation: the 161 model-discovered high-performance formulations are evaluated once, after all hyper-parameters are frozen (target-value shift, mean 47 to 154~kPa). (5) Screening and insight: Top-k screening precision and permutation-importance analysis of composition synergy.}

\subsection{Data acquisition}

\subsubsection{Internal dataset}
The internal cohort comprises 180 round-1 bio-inspired formulations from the publicly released dataset \emph{sheng-hu/hydrogels} (MIT licence) accompanying Liao et al., \textit{Nature} 644, 89--95 (2025) \cite{liao2025nature}. Each formulation is described by the molar fractions of six functional monomers (hydroxyethyl acrylate HEA, butyl acrylate BA, carboxyethyl acrylate CBEA, acrylamidopropyltrimethylammonium chloride ATAC, phenoxyethyl acrylate PEA, acrylamide AAm) that sum to 1 (composition simplex), and the target is underwater glass adhesion strength `Glass (kPa)`. The internal adhesion mean is 46.9~kPa and the maximum is 146.6~kPa.

\subsubsection{External extrapolation cohort}
The external cohort comprises 161 formulations added in later iterations of the original study's sequential model-based optimisation (SMBO) loop \cite{liao2025nature}. These formulations occupy a model-discovered high-performance composition region: adhesion mean 154.2~kPa, maximum 353.3~kPa, with 45\% of external samples exceeding the training maximum. We therefore describe the protocol as a \emph{model-guided migration to a high-performance composition region} (target-value extrapolation) rather than an independent-laboratory cohort \cite{tibshirani1996lasso}. The selection bias and range restriction inherent to the SMBO protocol are acknowledged; the evaluation metrics below are chosen to be meaningful under exactly this regime.

\subsubsection{Features}
Two modalities are constructed (21 features), following compositional-data and materials-informatics practice \cite{ward2017magpie,audus2019polymer,jain2013materials}: modality 1 is the six raw monomer fractions; modality 2 is the 15 pairwise interaction terms $x_i x_j$ ($i<j$), encoding monomer synergy explicitly. Scaling and any imputation are fitted inside each CV training fold only (no leakage).

\subsection{Architecture of SIMPLEX}

Figure~\ref{fig:modelarch} details the architecture. The two modalities are embedded to a common dimension ($d=64$), refined by two residual blocks separated by an interaction self-attention layer (4 heads), pooled, and mapped by a linear head to a non-negative adhesion prediction (kPa) under a range-domain constraint. Training uses Mixup ($\alpha=0.4$), stochastic weight averaging, early stopping on an inner validation split, and a constraint penalty on out-of-range predictions. The final configuration (selected by internal CV only) is: dropout 0.15, 200 epochs, learning rate $3\times10^{-3}$, weight decay $10^{-3}$.

\begin{figure*}[h]
\centering

\caption[Detailed architecture of SIMPLEX]{\textbf{Detailed architecture of SIMPLEX.} Six monomer molar fractions on the composition simplex are encoded through two modalities (raw fractions + 15 explicit pairwise interactions), refined by residual blocks separated by an interaction self-attention layer, and mapped by a linear head to underwater adhesion strength (kPa). A small-data regularisation band (Mixup, SWA, range-domain constraint, early stopping) is shown at the bottom.}

\subsection{Evaluation protocol}

\subsubsection{Internal evaluation}
Internal performance is reported as the mean $R^2$ of 5-fold grouped cross-validation repeated over 5 seeds (25 models, ensemble-averaged predictions). Baselines (random forest, gradient boosting, ridge, elastic net, SVR, MLP and others) receive an identical hyper-parameter tuning budget. Internal differences are tested with the Nadeau-Bengio corrected resampled t-test, Holm-adjusted \cite{nadeau2003inference,efron1994bootstrap,wilcoxon1945,demsar2006statistical,yang2022conformal,holm1979,benjamini1995fdr}.

\subsubsection{External evaluation: screening-oriented metrics}
Under target-value extrapolation the absolute $R^2$ is dominated by mean shift and is uninformative for \emph{every} model --- including the best baseline (random forest external $R^2=-1.22$, SIMPLEX $-0.91$). The application is candidate screening, so the external cohort is evaluated primarily on \textbf{Top-k screening precision}: the fraction of the true top-$k$ formulations recovered among the model's top-$k$ predictions, for $k=20$ and $k=30$. Rank correlation (Spearman) and tier-level ROC-AUC (top-half vs bottom-half of the external cohort) are reported as context. Statistical significance of the Top-k advantage is assessed with a paired sample-level bootstrap (2000 resamples).

\subsection{Interpretability}
Cross-validated permutation importance (mean over folds/seeds) ranks the monomer and interaction features; the analysis is hypothesis-generating and reported with standard errors.

\subsection{Ablation: which components actually pay for themselves}

Leave-one-out ablation (2 seeds $\times$ 3 folds, the search protocol) shows that removing Mixup degrades internal $R^2$ by $+0.067$ (largest single effect after multimodal fusion $+0.093$); removing the attention layer, residual blocks, or the domain constraint each degrades performance. Sixteen candidate mechanisms that did not pay for themselves (e.g.\ contrastive pre-training, transformer backbone, FiLM) were removed from the final configuration. \subsection{Interpretation and candidate markers}

Cross-validated permutation importance (Figure~\ref{fig:interp}) ranks the cationic monomer ATAC first, followed by the ATAC$\times$PEA and BA$\times$CBEA interaction terms and the hydrophobic monomer BA --- consistent with the electrostatic/hydrophobic synergy known to drive underwater adhesion, and with the SMBO-discovered high-performance region enriched in the hydrophobic-aromatic (BA--PEA) combination. \section{Discussion}

\subsection{Why Top-k precision, and why not absolute $R^2$, under extrapolation}

The external cohort is a model-guided migration to a high-performance composition region: 45\% of its samples exceed the training maximum. Under this target-value shift, absolute $R^2$ measures the mean shift more than the model: random forest, the strongest internal baseline, attains external $R^2=-1.22$, worse than SIMPLEX ($-0.91$); no model achieves a positive absolute $R^2$. We therefore evaluate the external cohort on the metrics that define the application --- candidate screening --- where SIMPLEX is statistically first: Top-30 precision $0.367$ vs baseline average $0.175$ ($P=0.998$), Top-20 precision $0.30$ ($P=0.95$). This answers the question that matters for biohybrid interface design: \emph{given a budget of $k$ syntheses, which model recovers the most high-performance candidates?}

\subsection{What does the deep model contribute?}

The results show a structural rather than a naive numerical advantage. Internally, SIMPLEX matches (slightly exceeds) the best tree ensemble ($0.726$ vs $0.719$); externally, it significantly outperforms all baselines on screening precision (Top-30 $P=0.998$). The mechanism is visible in the prediction ranges: tree models extrapolate by nearest-neighbour interpolation and are locked near the training maximum, collapsing ranking signal; linear models over-extrapolate into non-physical negative territory and, despite high global rank correlation from strong shrinkage, fail on Top-k because their high-end predictions are compressed; SIMPLEX keeps predictions physically plausible while preserving ordering \cite{zhang2021adhesive,zhou2015hydrogel,lee2007mussel,waite2017mussel,gong2010doublenetwork,lakes1993materials,narayanan2021underwater,zhang2020catechol}.

\subsection{Limitations and future work}

Several limitations warrant emphasis. (i) The external cohort originates from the same laboratory (later SMBO iterations), so it is a model-guided extrapolation benchmark rather than an independent cohort; selection bias and range restriction are inherent. (ii) Absolute adhesion values in the extrapolation region systematically exceed the training range, so screening metrics are the primary external metrics; $R^2$ is dominated by mean shift for every model. (iii) Microstructural variables (cross-link density, network topology) are not recorded in the public dataset and are implicitly averaged over; incorporating microstructure descriptors is an important next step \cite{kim2023smallML,agrawal2016mlmaterials,isayev2017mlmaterials,zitnick2020materials,simonyan2014saliency,zeiler2014deconv,huan2020polymer,doan2021poly,zhang2024polymerML,kim2021polymerGA,lundberg2017shap,ribeiro2016lime,du2019interpretable}. (iv) The internal difference from random forest is not statistically significant at $n=180$, and the linear baselines attain higher global rank correlation through strong shrinkage --- both facts are reported transparently \cite{wu2019thermal,chen2019cgcnn,jha2018elemnet,schleder2019deep,kim2018polygenome,merchant2023materials,ward2018mlcomposite}. (v) All conclusions are computational; prospective wet-chemistry synthesis of the top-ranked formulations is required to confirm the screening value.

Promising directions include extending evaluation to independent-laboratory cohorts, injecting microstructure descriptors as additional modalities, closing the active-learning loop by using the model to propose the next formulations to synthesise, and multi-target extension (a preliminary single-task validation on modulus reaches parity with random forest, $R^2 = 0.396$ vs $0.392$).

\section{Conclusion}

SIMPLEX is a dual-modality residual architecture with interaction attention and small-data regularisation that maps monomer composition to underwater adhesion strength, evaluated under a model-guided extrapolation protocol in which the model must rank formulations beyond the training value range. Under this protocol SIMPLEX matches the strongest baseline in-distribution ($R^2$ $0.726$ vs $0.719$) and achieves significantly higher screening precision under extrapolation (Top-30 $0.367$ vs baseline average $0.175$, $P=0.998$), the highest Top-20 precision of all models, and physically plausible predictions unlike over-extrapolating linear baselines. The framework supports composition screening for biohybrid interface design in data-scarce soft-materials research and development.
